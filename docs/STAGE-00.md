## Person, problem, and wish

**Person:** A parent of a prospective Minerva student, especially a
younger applicant who is 16 or 17.

**Previous framing, retained for comparison:** Most admissions information and
communication is directed at students. However, parents can also be
important stakeholders, especially for younger applicants. They may
have difficulty finding information about the admissions process,
important deadlines, and what they need to do.

This is the owner's starting observation, not an interview finding or
a verified claim about all Minerva communication. Public parent resources
do exist; see [the source note](MINERVA-SOURCES.md).

**Revised problem, following the owner's clarification on 2026-11-04:**
Minerva already has resources for parents. What is not yet clear to us
is how parents can participate in admissions itself, especially when
their child is 16 or 17: seeing relevant deadlines, understanding what
they need to do, and supporting their child without the applicant's account.
This is a question to investigate, not a claim that Minerva lacks a
parent participation process. The revision follows owner feedback,
not interviews. See the source note for the existing-page comparison.

**Wish:** Make it easier for parents to understand the process and
support their child without needing access to the applicant's account.

**Revised first-person draft for the owner to review:** I want to help
parents take part appropriately in their child's Minerva admissions
process, especially when their child is 16 or 17. I need something that
makes relevant deadlines and the parent's own steps clear, without
needing access to the applicant's account.

**Why a computer cannot simply solve it:** Finding public information is
mechanical. Deciding how to support a young applicant, what to discuss,
and what should stay private belongs to the parent and student. Whether
this captures the real difficulty still needs their stories.

**Computer-shaped version we are choosing not to build:** Track the
applicant's account and tell the parent what to do automatically.

## Privacy boundary

Week 1 uses official public information. It does not request applicant
credentials, access an applicant account, collect application status,
or collect financial documents. Reading public guidance is separate
from permission to see a student's private information.

Later, the owner would like to explore parents completing their own
part of financial aid separately, especially when they do not want to
share financial information with the student or another parent.
That is not part of Week 1. Existing confidentiality and separate
submission options must be confirmed with Minerva; this note promises
neither a new service nor a capability in Minerva's current systems.

## Three questions of the idea

These are agent proposals for the owner to correct, not owner-approved
answers or findings from conversations.

**Should it exist?** Only if parents have an unmet participation need
after considering Minerva's existing pages and processes. Ask Admissions
what exists and hear parents' stories before proposing a separate service.

**Who does it touch?** Parents, applicants, other parents or guardians,
Admissions and Financial Aid staff, and the people maintaining official
information. A parent should not gain access at the student's expense.

**What will people learn or stop learning?** Parents could learn the
process and where to verify it. An overly directive tool could discourage
them from talking with their child or asking Minerva about exceptions.

**Strongest case against it, from the agent:** A second information site
can become stale, look official, and increase parental pressure. It could
add confusion for students and work for Admissions. If official resources
already solve the problem, improve how parents find them instead.

## One proposed first activity

**Find one relevant deadline and discuss one next step with your child.**
A parent chooses an application round, opens its official process and
deadline sources, and identifies one thing to discuss with their child.
No login, personal details, application tracking, or financial upload.
This is a proposal, not an approved feature or product copy.

Begin with official links. Display copied dates only after confirming
cycle years, time zones, and binding exceptions. Any later displayed facts
need a source and checked date; unresolved facts stay visibly unresolved.
Use source wording or human-edited wording, not generated admissions advice.
No layout or implementation is specified in this stage.

Against the revised problem, this activity would help understanding;
it would not provide a parent account, official task completion, or
separate financial submission. Whether it is enough remains to be tested.
Until Admissions confirms parent responsibilities, do not assign parent
tasks or claim the Hub provides participation in Minerva's systems.

Applying `learning-orientation-design` to the proposed activity:

| Question | Provisional assessment |
|---|---|
| Deepens understanding? | Intended yes: connect a round and its source to a real next step. Must observe a parent using it. |
| Invites participation? | Intended yes: parent and child choose what to discuss. |
| Supports agency? | Intended yes: no account access or decisions on the student's behalf. |
| Clear rather than clever? | Intended yes: one activity and official links, no automated advice. |

These are design intentions, not proof that all four tests pass.
